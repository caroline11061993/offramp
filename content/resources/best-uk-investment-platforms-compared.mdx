Choosing an investment platform feels like it should be simple — they're all doing roughly the same job, moving your money into funds and shares — but the fee structures differ enough that the "wrong" choice can quietly cost you hundreds of pounds a year once your portfolio grows. There's no single "best" platform; the right one depends on how much you're investing, how often you trade, and how much you value things like research tools versus a rock-bottom fee.

Here's a straight comparison of the platforms that come up most often in UK personal finance conversations, based on their current published pricing.

*Quick disclaimer: fees and features change, sometimes without much notice, so always check the provider's own site for current pricing before committing. This isn't personal financial advice — it's a starting point for your own comparison.*

## The fee models, briefly

UK platforms generally charge one of two ways:

- **Percentage-based fees** — a small percentage of your total portfolio value each year. These scale with you: cheap on a small portfolio, more expensive as it grows (though many cap the fee at a maximum).
- **Flat monthly fees** — a fixed amount regardless of portfolio size. These start out looking more expensive on a small pot but become much better value as your investments grow.

Which model wins depends entirely on your portfolio size — a percentage fee that looks trivial at £5,000 can be meaningfully more expensive than a flat fee once you're past £40,000-£50,000.

## The platforms

### Hargreaves Lansdown

The largest and most established platform in the UK, and the one most people have heard of even if they've never invested. HL cut its platform fee to 0.35% (capped at £150/year per account type) from March 2026, following years of being the most expensive major platform.

**Strengths:** the widest fund research and selection, a genuinely excellent app and website, and customer service that's consistently rated above its competitors — you can actually get a human on the phone.

**Trade-off:** even after the fee cut, HL remains pricier than the newer, leaner platforms for larger portfolios. On a £100,000 portfolio, you'd hit the £150 cap — reasonable, but InvestEngine or Trading 212 would cost you nothing at that size.

**Best for:** investors who value research depth and support over shaving off the last few pounds of fees, especially those with SIPPs and multiple account types who benefit from everything being in one well-supported place.

### Trading 212

A commission-free app-based platform that's become one of the most popular entry points for newer UK investors, particularly for ISA investing.

**Strengths:** no platform fee and no dealing charges on its ISA or general investment account, fractional shares (so you can invest small amounts in expensive stocks), and a low 0.15% foreign exchange fee when buying overseas shares — among the cheapest of any platform.

**Trade-off:** lighter on research and educational tools compared to HL or AJ Bell, and no SIPP offering, so it doesn't cover your full account needs on its own.

**Best for:** cost-conscious ISA investors, especially those who want to start small or invest regularly without fees eating into contributions.

### AJ Bell

Sits comfortably between HL's premium positioning and the free neobrokers — a solid, broad-range platform without the top-tier price tag.

**Strengths:** a 0.25% platform fee (lower than HL's), a wide range of investments including both funds and shares, and a full suite of account types including SIPP, ISA, and general investing — genuinely one of the most complete "all your accounts in one place" options.

**Trade-off:** not the cheapest at any single price point, but reliably mid-market and dependable across the board.

**Best for:** investors who want more choice and flexibility than the zero-fee apps offer, without paying HL's premium.

### Vanguard UK

Vanguard's own platform, built primarily to hold Vanguard's own index funds — which happen to be some of the lowest-cost funds available.

**Strengths:** a straightforward, low-drama setup — a flat £4/month (£48/year) on balances under £32,000, then 0.15% up to a £375/year cap. Ideal for the "set up a direct debit into a global index fund and don't touch it" approach that a lot of long-term FIRE investing is built around.

**Trade-off:** limited to Vanguard's own fund range — you can't hold other providers' funds or individual shares here.

**Best for:** hands-off, buy-and-hold index investors who don't need fund choice beyond what Vanguard already offers.

### InvestEngine

A newer, ETF-focused platform that's built a strong reputation for its free "DIY" tier.

**Strengths:** no platform fee at all on the DIY plan, plus automatic portfolio rebalancing on new contributions — genuinely useful if you're running a multi-ETF portfolio and don't want to manually rebalance every time you add money.

**Trade-off:** ETFs only — no individual shares, and a shorter track record than the older, established names.

**Best for:** passive, ETF-based investors who want the auto-rebalancing without paying for it.

### Freetrade

Another commission-free app, historically known for its share-dealing focus, but it made a significant change in January 2026: ISA and SIPP access moved onto its free Basic plan, where they previously required a paid subscription.

**Strengths:** now offers ISA and SIPP for free, a clean app experience, and (on its paid Plus tier) 3.5% AER on uninvested cash up to £3,000.

**Trade-off:** paid tiers add real cost (£11.99/month for Plus) if you want the higher interest rate or more advanced features — worth checking whether the free tier covers what you actually need before assuming you need to pay.

**Best for:** investors who want free ISA/SIPP access with a simple app, without needing every advanced feature.

### Interactive Investor (ii)

A flat-fee platform aimed squarely at investors with meaningful portfolio size, where percentage fees start to bite.

**Strengths:** the Core plan (£5.99/month, roughly £72/year) covers portfolios up to £100,000 for a fixed cost regardless of how large your portfolio grows within that. Once your portfolio passes roughly £48,000, ii's flat fee typically becomes cheaper than Vanguard's percentage-based charge.

**Trade-off:** at smaller portfolio sizes, the flat fee is proportionally more expensive than percentage-based alternatives — this only pays off once you've built up a reasonable pot.

**Best for:** investors with £50,000+ already invested, where a flat fee clearly beats a percentage cut.

## How to actually decide

Rather than chasing the single cheapest platform, it's worth asking three questions: Do you need a SIPP as well as an ISA, or just one? Roughly how large is your portfolio likely to be in 2-3 years, not just today — since that determines whether a percentage or flat fee wins? And how much do you value research tools and support versus just wanting the lowest possible cost?

There's also nothing stopping you from splitting across platforms — plenty of investors hold, say, a Vanguard ISA for hands-off index investing and a Trading 212 account for smaller, more active picks. Since April 2024, you're free to open ISAs with multiple providers in the same tax year, as long as your total contributions across all of them stay within the £20,000 annual allowance.
